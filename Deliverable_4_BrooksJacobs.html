Introduction

This project uses atmospheric measurements collected by NASA during the 2010 GRIP mission. The goal is to examine how atmospheric pressure, temperature, and wind speed change at different altitudes. Before comparing these measurements, the dataset needs to be imported and checked for missing or unusual values.

Dataset Dimensions

The dataset contains atmospheric measurements collected during NASA’s 2010 GRIP mission. Each row represents an observation recorded by the dropsonde, while the columns represent different atmospheric measurements and related information.

The dataset contains 2310 observations and 18 variables, now including one altitude group variable created for this analysis.

Data Cleanliness

The dataset contains several missing measurements. Some variables, such as time, hour, minute, and second, have no missing values. However, pressure and temperature are each missing 1,205 observations (52.2%). Longitude, latitude, and GPS altitude each have 1,368 missing observations (59.2%).

Some missing measurements were originally represented by -999 instead of NA. These values had to be converted into NA so that R could recognize them as missing data. Rather than removing every row with missing values, I plan to keep the available measurements and only exclude missing values when necessary for calculations or graphs.

Discussion

The dataset shows that pressure and temperature decrease as altitude increases. Wind speed varies more, reaching nearly 25 m/s around 6,500 meters. This makes me interested in why winds are stronger at certain altitudes.

One concern is that some variables are missing over half their observations. I also had to replace invalid -999 values with NA. Moving forward, I want to explore how altitude, pressure, temperature, and wind speed relate to each other, especially what causes stronger winds at certain altitudes.

Overall Take

So far, I have learned that atmospheric pressure and temperature both decrease as altitude increases while wind speed changes more throughout the descent. One thing that surprised me was how much stronger the winds were at middle altitudes compared to lower and higher altitudes which the graphs helped me see these patterns better.

I still want to understand why wind speed reaches its highest values around 6,500 metrs and whether this is related to temperature or pressure. Another concern is the amount of missing data, which almost certainly affect the results. For my draft, I plan to investigate these relationships further and see if there are any other patterns worth exploring.
